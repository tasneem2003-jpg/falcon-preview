Science runs from elementary school through middle school. It is inquiry-based and follows the three-dimensional model of teaching and learning: concepts, skills, and content.

Elementary School KG – G5

Part of the Primary Years Programme ›

In the Classroom

  • Questioning
  • Predicting
  • Classifying
  • Measuring
  • Observing nature

In elementary school, students learn science through inquiry, play, and observing nature. They practice questioning, predicting, classifying, measuring, and reflecting, and explore ideas like change, systems, form, and cause and effect. Cross-subject units link science to daily life and caring for the environment, and build the thinking, communication, and research skills needed for middle school science.

Middle School G6 – G8

Part of the Middle Years Programme ›

Sample Projects

Lab InvestigationsPlan a test, collect data and explain the result.
Global-Issue ProjectsConnect science to real problems, like energy and the environment.
Scientific ReportsWrite up findings from a lab notebook.

In middle school, students study Earth and Space Science, Life Science, and Physical Science. Through experiments and cross-subject projects, they investigate real-world events and global issues. Building on what they know, they sharpen their scientific thinking, lab techniques, and data analysis, and keep exploring big ideas such as systems, relationships, and energy from a global view.

High School G9 – G12

In high school, students take Honors Biology, Chemistry, and Physics, then move on to AP Environmental Science, AP Physics C: Mechanics, and NOVA engineering tracks.

High School Courses ›

Learning by Doing

At both levels, students learn by doing: group experiments, models, debates, simulations, and design challenges. Lessons link to real life, and students lead their own work, get creative, and solve problems.

Assessment in Science

Teachers use formative and summative assessment, giving students several ways to show what they know. Ongoing checks include reflection journals, lab notebooks, peer assessment, and teacher feedback. Final assessments include scientific reports, multimedia presentations, investigations, and practical tasks.

By the End of Middle School

By the end of middle school, students understand core science concepts, can run their own investigations, and can judge scientific claims for themselves.
