A day in Grade 7

Middle schoolers move between subject teachers, but the day still has a steady shape.

[Times from the daily schedule]

  1. 1

    Morning

    Advisory: a steady start

    Grade 7 begins in advisory, a small group with one teacher who checks in on each student, helps them keep track of assignments and talks through anything on their mind.

    It is where middle schoolers learn to plan their own week, with an adult who knows them well close by.

  2. 2

    Late morning

    Classes across the eight subject groups

    Students move between subject teachers: language and literature, mathematics, sciences, individuals and societies, design and more.

    Lessons start from real questions, and work is assessed against clear criteria, so students know exactly what excellent work looks like and how to get there.

  3. 3

    Midday

    Prayer and lunch

    Students pray together, then have lunch with friends. Families can pre-order a healthy, halal school lunch each month or send lunch from home.

  4. 4

    Afternoon

    Arabic and Islamic Studies

    Arabic language, Quran and Islamic Studies are part of every day, taught by specialist teachers. Students grow in reading, writing and speaking Arabic, and explore what their faith means in their own lives.

    School-wide events such as the Annual Quran Competition give them something to work toward.

    Arabic & Islamic Studies by grade →
  5. 5

    After school

    Clubs, enrichment and service

    Optional after-school programs include Quran memorization, conversational Arabic, homework support, and coding and robotics. Choose the full week or selected days in the Edumagined parent portal.

    Service as Action projects take students beyond the classroom, like this neighborhood litter clean-up. On Sundays, boys ages 11–15 can also join Sabiqoon at the Ashburn campus.

Eight subject groups

The MYP keeps learning broad: students study all eight IB subject groups every year, so no door closes too early.

Language and literature

At Falcon: English Language Arts

Reading closely, analyzing texts and writing in many forms.

Language acquisition

At Falcon: Arabic Language

Building fluency in reading, writing, speaking and listening.

Individuals and societies

At Falcon: Social Studies

History, geography and civics, through real questions about people and places.

Sciences

At Falcon: Science

Hands-on investigation: asking a question, testing it and explaining the result.

Mathematics

At Falcon: Mathematics

Number, algebra, geometry and data, with reasoning explained step by step.

Arts

At Falcon: Visual Arts

Developing skills, ideas and a personal style.

Physical and health education

At Falcon: Physical & Health Education

Fitness, teamwork and healthy choices.

Design

At Falcon: Computer Science, IT & Design

Coding and solving real problems with the design cycle.

Plus, every day

Islamic Studies and Quran

Faith and values, taught in a way that is age-appropriate and personally meaningful.

The Personal Project and Service as Action

Two parts of the MYP that parents often ask about, explained simply.

In the final MYP year

The Personal Project

Each student picks something they truly care about: a skill to master, a product to build or a question to answer. Over several months they plan it, create it and reflect on what they learned, with a teacher as their supervisor.

It teaches the planning and self-management that high school and college will ask of them.

Every year

Service as Action

Students put what they learn to work for others: in school, in the neighborhood and beyond. It is not about logging hours. It is about noticing a real need and doing something about it.
