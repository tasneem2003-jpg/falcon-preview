A day in Grade 3

Every class has its own rhythm, but most days in the Primary Years follow this shape.

[Times from the daily schedule]

  1. 1

    Morning

    Homeroom circle, then the unit of inquiry

    The day begins with your child's homeroom teacher. The class gathers in a circle to greet each other, share news and look at the plan for the day.

    Then comes the unit of inquiry: a few weeks spent on one big question, such as how plants grow or why people move. Reading, writing, science and social studies all feed into it, so learning feels connected.

  2. 2

    Late morning

    Arabic and mathematics

    A specialist Arabic teacher takes the class for reading, writing and speaking practice. Letters and sounds become words, then sentences, then short stories.

    Mathematics follows: hands-on work with numbers, shapes and measurement, then practice in small groups so the teacher can see who needs help and who is ready for more.

  3. 3

    Midday

    Lunch, outdoor play and prayer

    Children eat together. Families can pre-order a healthy, halal school lunch each month or pack lunch from home.

    Then it is time to run. The Chantilly campus has a playground, a fenced play area, a soccer field, and basketball, volleyball and badminton courts. The class comes back together for prayer before the afternoon begins.

  4. 4

    Afternoon

    Quran, Islamic Studies and specials

    Afternoons include Quran and Islamic Studies, taught in a way that is age-appropriate and personally meaningful, through stories, values, prayer and memorization.

    Through the week your child also has specials with specialist teachers: art, physical education and computer science.

Six big themes, explored every year

The PYP organizes learning around six transdisciplinary themes. Each year, children explore every theme through units of inquiry that bring several subjects together.

Who we are

Identity, beliefs and values, health and relationships.

Example unit

What makes a good friend and a caring classmate?

Where we are in place and time

History, journeys, homes and discoveries.

Example unit

How have our families' journeys shaped who we are today?

How we express ourselves

Ideas, feelings, culture and creativity.

Example unit

How do stories and poems, in English and Arabic, share what we feel?

How the world works

The natural world, science and technology.

Example unit

How can wind and sunlight be turned into energy?

How we organize ourselves

Communities, systems and how people work together.

Example unit

How does a market work, from making something to selling it?

Sharing the planet

Caring for the earth and sharing it fairly.

Example unit

How can we cut waste at school and at home?

Example units show the kind of question a child might explore; they are not the actual unit list. [Programme of inquiry by grade]
