Dual Enrollment with NOVA

Falcon high school students can take college courses at Northern Virginia Community College (NOVA) and earn college credit before they graduate, with guidance from our school counseling team.

What dual enrollment is

Students stay enrolled at Falcon Academy and, at the same time, enroll on their own in approved NOVA courses. Depending on the course, they may earn both college credit and high school credit.

This is independent dual enrollment: the student enrolls directly with NOVA and takes NOVA’s classes. It is not a college class taught at Falcon.

FWhat Falcon does

  • Helps your child choose courses that fit their goals
  • Checks how each course fits the high school plan
  • Decides whether a NOVA course also counts for Falcon credit
  • Helps students balance high school and college work

NWhat NOVA does

  • Admission decisions and course eligibility
  • Placement and registration
  • Tuition and fees
  • Teaching, grading and the college transcript

Why it helps

Students experience real college learning while they still have the support of their high school.

Earn college credit early

Start a college transcript and, in some cases, cover degree requirements before university.

Stronger college applications

Show initiative and the ability to succeed in college-level work. It does not guarantee admission.

A personal pathway

Counselors and families build a plan that fits each student’s interests and intended major.

An associate degree, for some

Students who plan carefully may complete some or all of an associate degree. It is optional and not guaranteed.

Save time and money

Credits that transfer can shorten university study and reduce tuition later.

Ready for college life

Independent learning, time management and accountability, practiced with support.

Who can join

Students who want to take NOVA courses should meet all six of these expectations.

  1. 1Stay enrolled at FalconAnd keep working toward high school graduation requirements.
  2. 2Meet NOVA’s admission requirementsNOVA’s rules for high school students taking college courses.
  3. 3Show college-level readinessThrough school records, test scores, placement measures or prerequisite courses.
  4. 4Get counselor approvalMeet a Falcon counselor before registering for any NOVA course.
  5. 5Meet course prerequisitesSome NOVA courses require earlier coursework or proven skills.
  6. 6Keep good progressDo well in both Falcon and NOVA courses, and meet college deadlines.

English and math placement

Many NOVA courses need proof that a student is ready for college-level English or math. NOVA sets the rules and the scores, and they can change.

English readiness

May be shown through:

  • High school grades and coursework
  • SAT or ACT scores
  • AP exam results
  • IB exam results
  • Other measures NOVA accepts

Math readiness

May be shown through:

  • High school math coursework
  • Standardized test scores
  • AP or IB math exam results
  • Earlier college math courses
  • Other measures NOVA accepts

Calculus and higher-level courses may need extra prerequisites.

Frequently Asked Questions

From which grade can my child start?
Eligible students may take NOVA courses as early as Grade 9, if they meet NOVA’s admission rules and course requirements.
Will NOVA courses count toward my child’s Falcon diploma?
Only if Falcon approves it. A NOVA course does not count automatically, so your child should confirm with a counselor before registering.
Will the credits transfer to any university?
Not always. The university your child attends decides which credits it accepts. Check the transfer policies of the colleges on your list.
Does dual enrollment guarantee college admission?
No. It shows readiness for college-level work, which can strengthen an application.
Can my child earn an associate degree?
Some students may, with careful planning with Falcon counselors and NOVA advisors. It depends on course availability, eligibility, grades and time, and it is not guaranteed.
Who pays for NOVA courses?
