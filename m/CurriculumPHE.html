At Falcon Academy, Physical and Health Education helps students stay well, move with skill and make healthy decisions. In elementary and middle school, it covers fitness, teamwork and healthy habits, in classes where every student takes part.

Read moreShow less

Lessons follow the three-dimensional model of teaching and learning (concepts, skills, and content). Students learn why staying active matters and how to look after their health.

Elementary School KG – G5

In the Classroom

  • Running, jumping, throwing
  • Dance
  • Fitness challenges
  • Cooperative games
  • Healthy choices

Primary students build basic movement skills such as running, jumping, throwing and catching, plus coordination, balance and spatial awareness through games and activities. They play games, work in teams and practice on their own, so they enjoy moving and grow in confidence.

Read moreShow less

Health lessons cover personal hygiene, nutrition, emotions, safety and community well-being. Students explore questions like “How does my body work?” and “Why is exercise important for my health?”

Activities include structured games, basic fitness challenges, dance and free movement, plus lessons on managing emotions, nutrition and healthy choices. Students learn to work together, set goals, manage themselves and speak respectfully.

Teachers watch students in class and use self-reflection, peer feedback and skill demonstrations. Final tasks may include fitness journals, personal goal portfolios and class challenges where students use what they learned.

In middle school, students move on to team sports, individual fitness training, aquatics and outdoor activities, with more strategy and specialist skill. They study the science behind movement, anatomy, physiology and health-related fitness.

Read moreShow less

Health topics grow to include mental health, substance abuse prevention, nutrition science and lifestyle choices that affect long-term health. Students learn how exercise and healthy habits affect their own health and their community’s.

Students do skill drills, tactical games, fitness assessments and health promotion projects. Real-life challenges build critical thinking, decision-making, leadership and resilience.

Teachers check progress with skill feedback, fitness tests, self and peer assessments and reflective journals. Final tasks include designing and running a fitness program, leading a team activity, creating a health campaign, or researching and presenting a health topic.

The goal is students who stay active, know how to look after their health, and make good choices for themselves and their community.
