Academic Honesty Policy

How students, staff and families keep schoolwork honest at Falcon, from Kindergarten to Grade 8.

At a glance

  • Students hand in their own work and credit every source, including AI tools.
  • Plagiarism, collusion and handing in the same work twice all count as misconduct.
  • Families support learning but do not complete assignments for their child.
  • A first incident usually means a redo and a call home. Repeated incidents go to the principal.

What academic integrity means

At Falcon, we work together as a school community to keep learning honest. Administration, staff, students and families all share the responsibility to understand and follow this policy.

“Academic integrity is a guiding principle in education and a choice to act in a responsible way whereby others can have trust in us as individuals.”International Baccalaureate Organization

In practice, students learn to:

  • produce authentic individual and group work
  • credit the sources, work and ideas of others correctly
  • use technology and social media responsibly
  • act honestly during tests and examinations

These habits connect to the IB Learner Profile (especially being principled) and to the Approaches to Learning skills: thinking, research, communication, social and self-management.

What counts as misconduct

Plagiarism

Presenting another person’s ideas or work as your own.

Collusion

Letting another student copy your work or hand it in as theirs.

Duplication

Handing in the same work for different assignments.

Other examples: cheating or copying, falsifying data or records, helping someone else be dishonest, and deliberately interfering with another student’s work.

Who does what

Students

  • Follow the policy and ask when unsure
  • Say when AI was used in their work
  • Report misconduct they see

Families

  • Read and support the policy
  • Help children find and credit sources
  • Let children do their own assignments

Staff

  • Teach citation, quoting and paraphrasing
  • Model honest practice
  • Respond with a restorative approach

What happens if it occurs

Elementary school (KG–Grade 5)

Consequences match how serious the incident is and the child’s age and understanding. They may include contacting the family, redoing the work or a Behavior Ticket. Repeat incidents lead to stronger consequences.

Middle school (Grades 6–8)

  1. 1The teacher reports the incident and tells the family.
  2. 2The student gets a chance to redo the task.
  3. 3Self-management and work-completion scores are reduced.
  4. 4Repeated incidents are referred to the principal.

How we monitor and support

Middle school teachers monitor classroom computer use, check formative and summative work, and oversee group work. If an incident happens, it is discussed at grade-level team or academic support committee meetings so all teachers can support the student.

  • Separate student Wi-Fi with content filtering, plus a filter on every student laptop
  • Securly to monitor online use during class time
  • Citation help through Google Scholar and the Purdue Writing Lab
